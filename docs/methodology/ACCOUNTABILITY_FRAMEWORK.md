# Accountability Framework

Residents are usually told to "call someone." They are rarely told **who can actually do the
thing they want done**. This framework exists to answer that honestly — which means
admitting, often, that responsibility is split and that no single office controls the
outcome.

**Not built.** This is the specification for the "Who Is Responsible" page.

## Types of authority

Every office is mapped to a problem with **one of four** relationships. Naming the type is
as important as naming the office.

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Direct authority** | Can decide and execute the action themselves |
| **Budget authority** | Controls the money that makes the action possible, but does not execute it |
| **Coordination influence** | Can convene, pressure, escalate, or prioritize — but cannot compel |
| **No direct control** | Frequently blamed, genuinely cannot deliver the outcome |

The fourth category is deliberate. Telling a resident that their alderman cannot prosecute a
case is more useful than letting them spend a year asking.

## Offices

| Office | Typical relationship | What they actually control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Alderman** | Coordination influence; budget authority (menu funds) | Ward-level infrastructure spending, service escalation, convening, ordinances |
| **Mayor** | Budget authority; direct authority over departments | Department leadership, citywide budget priorities |
| **Chicago Police Department** | Direct authority | Patrol deployment, investigation, arrests |
| **District commander** | Direct authority (local) | Beat deployment, local strategy; answers at beat meetings |
| **Cook County State's Attorney** | Direct authority | Whether to charge, what to charge, plea decisions |
| **Courts and judges** | Direct authority | Bond, pretrial release, sentencing |
| **Cook County Sheriff** | Direct authority | Jail, court security, some patrol |
| **Department of Buildings** | Direct authority | Vacant/dangerous buildings, code enforcement |
| **Chicago Dept. of Transportation** | Direct authority | Streets, signals, **street lighting** |
| **Streets and Sanitation** | Direct authority | Garbage, graffiti, debris, some lighting requests |
| **Federal and state agencies** | Varies; often no direct control locally | Grants, statutes, specific federal offenses |

Relationships are **typical**, not absolute; they vary by problem and must be stated per
problem on the page, not assumed from this table.

## Worked example: repeated work-van break-ins

A contractor's vans are broken into repeatedly and their tools stolen. Who is responsible?

There is no single answer, and pretending otherwise is the failure mode this page exists to
prevent.

| Office | Relationship here | What they can actually do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CPD district commander** | **Direct authority** | Shift patrol to the block and hours where break-ins cluster; assign investigation |
| **CPD (department)** | Direct authority | Investigate; connect incidents into a pattern rather than filing each alone |
| **Alderman** | **Coordination influence** + budget authority | Escalate to the district; fund lighting or cameras from menu money; convene neighbors |
| **CDOT** | **Direct authority** | Fix or add **street lighting** on the block — often the concrete change that matters |
| **Streets and Sanitation** | Direct authority | Clear debris and dumping that make a block feel and function as unwatched |
| **Department of Buildings** | Direct authority | Secure the vacant building next to the lot, if one is involved |
| **Cook County State's Attorney** | Direct authority | Decide whether to charge anyone arrested — the police cannot make this decision |
| **Courts** | Direct authority | Bond and sentencing outcomes — **not** controlled by police or the alderman |
| **Mayor** | Budget authority | Citywide staffing and budget, which set the ceiling on everything above |

**What this shows the resident:**

- The **fastest concrete lever** may be lighting (CDOT) or a vacant-property fix (Buildings)
  — not the office they were about to call.
- The **police can arrest but cannot charge**; the **State's Attorney can charge but does not
  patrol**; the **courts decide release and sentence** and answer to neither.
- Their **alderman cannot direct police deployment**, but *can* escalate and *can* fund
  lighting. Asking them for the first wastes a year; asking for the second may work.
- If break-ins continue after arrests, the reason may lie with any of several offices — and
  the site should show that distribution rather than manufacturing a villain.

## Rules

- Show the **distribution** of responsibility, not a single name to blame.
- State the **type** of authority every time; "responsible" without a type is misleading.
- **Do not assign authority an office does not have**, even when public frustration points
  there. An inaccurate mapping is unfair to the official and useless to the resident.
- Public commitments are tracked with **evidence links**. `unclear` is a legitimate status
  and is always preferable to guessing.
- The framework describes **who can act**. It does not assert that any office **caused** an
  outcome — see [EVENTS_AND_POLICY_CONTEXT.md](EVENTS_AND_POLICY_CONTEXT.md).
